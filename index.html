Camera angles & shot sizes
Camera level — how high the camera sits relative to the subject
Overhead
straight down from above — lays out geography and scale, or disorients on purpose.
Eye level
matches how people actually look at each other — neutral, natural.
Shoulder level
a touch below eye level — common for handheld, observational coverage.
Hip level
around waist height, angled slightly up — common alongside a walking subject.
Knee level
low, looking further up — adds height and a little power to the subject.
Ground level
at the floor — makes a subject tower overhead, or reads action happening low.
Shot size — how much of the subject the frame holds, tightest to widest
Extreme close-up (ECU)
just the eyes or mouth — intense emotion, or a tiny detail.
Close-up (CU)
the face, forehead to chin — reaction and emotion.
Medium close-up (MCU)
head and upper chest — the standard dialogue frame.
Medium shot (MS)
waist up — balances expression with body language.
Cowboy shot (CS)
mid-thigh up, named for a Western gunslinger's holster — body language plus a hint of the surroundings.
Medium full shot (MFS)
knees up.
Full shot (FS)
head to toe — posture, movement, and the subject's relationship to the space around them.
These describe how a shot's prompt is framed — mention them in the shot prompt itself (e.g. "a cowboy shot, hip level") the same way you'd describe a camera move.
