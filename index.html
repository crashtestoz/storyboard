Camera prompt guide
Camera level — how high the camera sits relative to the subject
Overhead
straight down from above — lays out geography and scale, or disorients on purpose.
Eye level
matches how people actually look at each other — neutral, natural.
Shoulder level
a touch below eye level — common for handheld, observational coverage.
Hip level
around waist height, angled slightly up — common alongside a walking subject.
Knee level
low, looking further up — adds height and a little power to the subject.
Ground level
at the floor — makes a subject tower overhead, or reads action happening low.
Shot size — how much of the subject the frame holds, tightest to widest
Extreme close-up (ECU)
just the eyes or mouth — intense emotion, or a tiny detail.
Close-up (CU)
the face, forehead to chin — reaction and emotion.
Medium close-up (MCU)
head and upper chest — the standard dialogue frame.
Medium shot (MS)
waist up — balances expression with body language.
Cowboy shot (CS)
mid-thigh up, named for a Western gunslinger's holster — body language plus a hint of the surroundings.
Medium full shot (MFS)
knees up.
Full shot (FS)
head to toe — posture, movement, and the subject's relationship to the space around them.
Camera movement — how the camera itself moves during the shot, not what it sees
Dolly
move the whole camera toward or away from the subject — not a zoom, the camera itself travels.
Pedestal
raise or lower the whole camera, keeping it level.
Truck
move the whole camera sideways, left or right, at the same distance from the subject.
Pan
swivel the camera left or right from a fixed spot, like turning your head.
Tilt
swivel the camera up or down from a fixed spot, like nodding.
Roll
rotate the camera around the lens axis, tilting the horizon — used sparingly, for disorientation or a stylised look.
Turn the idea into a prompt — describe where the camera starts, how it moves, and where it ends

H3 responds more reliably when each part has one clear job. Use this order: starting position + movement and direction + angle or height + ending framing + speed.

Starting positionfront, side, behind, or front three-quarter; say whose left or right when it matters.
Camera heighteye level, shoulder level, hip level, knee level, or ground level.
Movementpan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal, orbit, or roll. Say the direction in plain language.
Framingname the shot size at the start and/or end: close-up, medium shot, full shot.
Timingslow, steady, or fast; add small, medium, or large amplitude when useful.
Important direction choice: “Counterclockwise” is clearest for an orbit around a subject or a roll of the horizon. For a horizontal pan, say “from screen left to screen right” or “from screen right to screen left.” “Zoom out” changes the lens; “dolly out” moves the camera physically away.
Pan with a framing change Eye-level front three-quarter medium shot from the subject's left. The camera pans from screen left to screen right with medium amplitude at slow speed, ending in a medium close-up.
Counterclockwise orbit and zoom out Begin at eye level in a medium shot with the subject centered. The camera slowly orbits counterclockwise around the subject while zooming out, ending in a full shot that reveals more of the surroundings.
Low angle and dolly in Start at knee level in a low-angle full shot behind the subject. Dolly forward with medium amplitude at steady speed, keeping the subject centered and ending in a medium shot.
Side movement and tilt Start at hip level in a side-on medium shot. Truck from left to right as the camera tilts upward slowly, ending on a medium close-up of the subject's face.
Rotation of the horizon Hold a close-up at eye level, then roll the camera counterclockwise slowly with small amplitude while the subject remains still.

Avoid combining “counterclockwise pan” with “zoom to medium shot.” Choose pan left/right, orbit counterclockwise, or roll counterclockwise, then state whether the framing gets tighter or wider.

Copy the pattern into a shot prompt: starting position → movement and direction → camera height/angle → ending shot size → speed.
